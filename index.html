3. DISCRETE MATHEMATICS (SETS & RELATIONS)

FUNDAMENTALS OF SET THEORY, RELATIONS, AND SYMMETRIC PROPERTY VERIFICATION.

📌 1. SETS & TYPES

Definition: A Set is a well-defined collection of distinct objects.

Example: A = {1, 2, 3, 4}

Types of Sets:

  • Finite Set: Elements can be counted. Example: A = {a, b, c}
  • Infinite Set: Elements cannot be counted. Example: N = {1, 2, 3, ...}
  • Empty Set (∅): A set with no elements. Example: A = {}
  • Subset (A ⊆ B): Every element of A is also in B.

📌 2. RELATIONS & TYPES

Definition: A relation R from set A to set B is a subset of the Cartesian Product (A × B).

Example: If A = {1, 2}, then A × A = {(1,1), (1,2), (2,1), (2,2)}.

Key Properties of Relations:

  • Reflexive: For every a ∈ A, (a, a) ∈ R.
  • Symmetric: If (a, b) ∈ R, then (b, a) ∈ R must also be in R.
  • Transitive: If (a, b) ∈ R and (b, c) ∈ R, then (a, c) ∈ R.

🧪 3. SOLVED PRACTICE QUESTION: SYMMETRIC PROPERTY

Question: Given set A = {1, 2, 3} and relation R = {(1,2), (2,1), (1,3), (3,1)}. Verify if the relation R is Symmetric.

Condition for Symmetric Relation: For every pair (a, b) ∈ R, the reverse pair (b, a) must also belong to R.

  1. Check Pair 1:
    Here pair (1,2) is present in R, and its reverse pair (2,1) is also present in R.

    ✅ Pair (1,2) has its reverse (2,1)!

  2. Check Pair 2:
    Here pair (1,3) is present in R, and its reverse pair (3,1) is also present in R.

    ✅ Pair (1,3) has its reverse (3,1)!

Conclusion: Since every ordered pair in R has its corresponding reverse pair, the relation R is Symmetric! 🎉

4. FUNCTIONAL ENGLISH (COMPLETE GRAMMAR MASTERCLASS)

MASTERING PHRASES, CLAUSES, SENTENCES, FRAGMENTS, RUN-ONS, MODIFIERS, TENSES, AND SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT.

📌 1. PHRASES, CLAUSES & SENTENCES

A. Phrase: A group of words that works together but does not contain a subject and a verb combination. It cannot stand alone as a sentence.
Example: "in the university library" (No subject doing the action).

B. Clause: A group of words that does contain both a Subject and a Verb.
* Independent Clause: Expresses a complete thought. Example: "Nimra studies hard."
* Dependent Clause: Starts with a subordinator, incomplete thought. Example: "Because she wants a scholarship."

C. Sentence: A complete grammatical unit made of one or more clauses, expressing a full thought with proper punctuation.
Example: "Nimra studies hard because she wants a scholarship."

📌 2. SENTENCE FRAGMENTS & RUN-ON SENTENCES

A. Sentence Fragment: An incomplete sentence written as if it were complete (missing a subject, verb, or complete thought).
Incorrect/Fragment: "Running towards the exam hall."
Correction: "Nimra is running towards the exam hall."

B. Run-on Sentence: Two or more independent clauses joined together incorrectly without proper punctuation or conjunctions.
Incorrect/Run-on: "She loves coding she practices every day."
Correction: "She loves coding, and she practices every day."

📌 3. MODIFIERS (ADJECTIVES & ADVERBS)

Definition: Words, phrases, or clauses that provide description, detail, or limitation to other words in a sentence.

  • Adjective Modifier: Modifies a noun.
    Example: "She wrote a brilliant C++ code." (Brilliant describes code).
  • Adverb Modifier: Modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb.
    Example: "She codes efficiently." (Efficiently describes how she codes).

📌 4. ENGLISH TENSES OVERVIEW

Tenses show the time of an action (Present, Past, Future) and its state (Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous).

  • Present Simple (Habits/Facts):
    Example: "Nimra learns algorithms daily."
  • Past Simple (Completed Actions):
    Example: "She completed her intermediate exams in June."
  • Future Simple (Upcoming Goals):
    Example: "She will secure the master's scholarship in Seoul."

📌 5. SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT

Golden Rule: A singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb.

  1. Singular Example:
    "She writes clean code." (Singular subject 'She' takes singular verb 'writes').
  2. Plural Example:
    "They write clean code." (Plural subject 'They' takes plural verb 'write').

Conclusion: Mastering these core concepts ensures flawless professional writing and strong academic presentation! 🎉

5. ECONOMICS (DETAILED MASTERY & FOUNDATIONAL THEORIES)

IN-DEPTH EXPLORATION OF ECONOMISTS, WEALTH, WELFARE, SCARCITY, AND THE FOUR PILLARS OF ECONOMIC SCIENCE.

📌 1. DETAILED VIEWS OF FAMOUS ECONOMISTS

A. Adam Smith (Father of Economics - Classical School):
* Book Name: "An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations" (Published in 1776).
* Detailed Explanation: Adam Smith defined Economics as the "Science of Wealth". He focused on how wealth is produced, consumed, exchanged, and accumulated by nations. He introduced the concept of the Invisible Hand (free market mechanism without state interference) and emphasized that the Division of Labor greatly increases output and economic growth.

B. Alfred Marshall (Neo-Classical School):
* Book Name: "Principles of Economics" (Published in 1890).
* Detailed Explanation: Marshall shifted the central focus of Economics from pure wealth to Human Welfare. He defined Economics as "a study of mankind in the ordinary business of life." According to Marshall, wealth is not the ultimate goal; rather, it is a means to achieve material well-being and satisfaction for human society.

C. Lionel Robbins (Modern Analytical Economist):
* Book Name: "An Essay on the Nature and Significance of Economic Science" (Published in 1932).
* Detailed Explanation: Robbins criticized earlier definitions and provided a neutral, analytical definition based on Scarcity and Choice. He stated: "Economics is the science which studies human behavior as a relationship between ends (unlimited wants) and scarce means (limited resources) which have alternative uses."

📌 2. THE FOUR PILLARS OF ECONOMIC ACTIVITY

  • Consumption: The direct utilization of goods and services to satisfy human desires. It is the beginning and ultimate end-point of all economic production.
  • Production: The creation of utility or value by combining Factors of Production (Land, Labor, Capital, and Entrepreneurship) to create goods and services.
  • Exchange: The buying and selling of goods, services, or assets between buyers and sellers, facilitated through money and market price mechanisms.
  • Distribution of Wealth: How national income and overall wealth are divided among workers (wages), landowners (rent), capitalists (interest), and entrepreneurs (profit).

📌 3. SCARCITY PROBLEMS, DEMAND & SUPPLY

A. The Basic Economic Problem (Scarcity):
Because human needs are infinite while resources (time, raw materials, capital) are limited, every society faces three fundamental economic questions: What to produce? How to produce? For whom to produce?

B. Demand and Supply Dynamics:
* Law of Demand: Higher prices lead to lower quantity demanded by consumers (inverse relationship).
* Law of Supply: Higher prices encourage producers to supply higher quantities to maximize profits (direct relationship).
* Equilibrium Point: The exact market balance where Quantity Demanded equals Quantity Supplied.

🧪 4. SOLVED PRACTICE: COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS OF THEORIES

Question: How does Lionel Robbins' definition differ fundamentally from Adam Smith's?

  1. Adam Smith's Focus: Concentrates on Wealth generation and accumulation as the primary goal of economics.
  2. Lionel Robbins' Focus: Concentrates on Scarcity of resources and decision-making behavior, making economics a universal science of choice.

Summary: While Smith viewed economics through physical wealth, Robbins elevated it to an analytical science of managing scarce resources! 🎉
